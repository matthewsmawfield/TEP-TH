11.1 Falsifiable Predictions

The following near-term observational tests would strengthen or falsify the Temporal Horizon Cosmology framework.

  1. Tensor action and transition-profile test. The assigned pump integrations provide cross-checked conditional diagnostics, with the fiducial values reported in Section 10.6. The tensor normalization of the shared action must first be matched to that pump before those values become physical B-mode predictions. The one-parameter scan does not bound every joint profile combination: $p=0.5$, $n=2$, $\eta_0=3000$ Mpc already yields a diagnostic maximum near $1.14\times10^{-4}$. The earlier universal $r\gtrsim10^{-4}$ exclusion threshold is therefore withdrawn. A quantitative observational threshold requires an action-normalized spectrum and a specified joint parameter domain.
  2. 21-cm power spectrum at cosmic dawn. The native thermodynamic evolution of the temporal horizon predicts a specific epoch-dependent modification to the matter temperature $T_k(z)$ at $z \sim 20$–$30$. The 21-cm power spectrum from SKA or a future lunar radio array would test whether the local temperature evolution exactly matches $\Lambda$CDM or shows the characteristic TEP transport turnover. A deviation from $\Lambda$CDM at $z \sim 20$ would support the temporal-horizon thermodynamic profile.
  3. Gravitational-wave propagation speed variation. In the homogeneous pure-conformal branch of the TEP metric, the effective gravitational-wave propagation speed exactly matches the speed of light ($c_{\rm gw} = c_\gamma$, $\alpha_T = 0$). Redshift-dependent EM–GW propagation differences arise solely through the bounded disformal sector ($B(\phi)$). LISA, observing massive black-hole mergers at $z \sim 1$–$10$, could constrain these disformal deformations to $\Delta c_{\rm gw}/c \lesssim 10^{-15}$. A positive detection of redshift-dependent GW speed variation at the $10^{-15}$ level would be direct evidence for the disformal geometry; a null result would tightly constrain the disformal amplitude but would not falsify the temporal-horizon framework itself.
  4. Local excitation temperature at depth. Rotational excitation of CN, CO and C I in high-redshift quasar absorption systems thermometers the ambient radiation field at the absorber's own location. The temporal-horizon law returns $T_{\rm loc}(z) = T_0/A_{\rm clock}(z) = T_0(1+z)$ natively at every depth, so the standard $T_{\rm CMB}(z)=T_0(1+z)$ excitation profile is a hard prediction requiring no transport assumption. Step 11 confronts it with the published CO absorber sample ($z=0.89$–$2.69$): $\chi^2/{\rm dof}=0.15$ ($\chi^2=1.08$), fitted exponent $\beta=+0.003\pm0.023$, versus $\chi^2=865.8$ for the constant-temperature null (descriptive $\sqrt{\Delta\chi^2}=29.4$) — the tested temperature law is corroborated on real data. Because local collisional excitation or ultraviolet pumping in dense clouds can only elevate $T_{\rm ex}$ above the ambient radiation bath ($T_{\rm ex}\ge T_{\rm loc}(z)$), the quiescent lower envelope across the absorber population must track $T_0(1+z)$; a systematic departure of that quiescent floor below $T_0(1+z)$ toward $T_0$ would discriminate directly against the local-temperature law, whereas continued confirmation of the $T_0(1+z)$ floor at $z\gtrsim3$ extends the depth over which the clock map is observationally validated.

Tensor and 21-cm observations are prospective tests whose quantitative TEP predictions require the action and thermal-profile matching described above. GW–EM propagation tests constrain the disformal sector; a null result does not exclude the pure-conformal boundary geometry. Excitation-temperature measurements probe the local thermal law directly and are the sharpest existing discriminator between a temporal-depth temperature gradient and any mechanism in which the ambient radiation field stays at $T_0$ along the path.
